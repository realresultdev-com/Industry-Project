The Industrial Problem: The Annotation Bottleneck

Manufacturing Realities:

  • High-Speed Production: Steel rolling lines exceed 15 meters/second; manual human inspection is fatigued, subjective, and prone to severe oversights.
  • The Defect Scarcity Paradox: Surface defects occur in less than 1% of total production volume.
  • Prohibitive Labeling Costs: Classifying micro-cracks and roll scale requires certified metallurgists and quality control engineers.
Why Supervised Deep Learning Fails:

Deep CNNs (ResNet-18/50) contain 11M to 25M parameters. When trained on small labeled industrial sets (10 to 50 samples):

  • Catastrophic overfitting to background lighting noise.
  • Zero generalization to new production batches.
  • Massive performance collapse under label scarcity.
Speaker Script: "In high-throughput manufacturing, defects are rare but catastrophic. Supervised deep learning models achieve high accuracy only when given thousands of labeled defect examples. When standard CNNs are trained on only 10 or 20 defect samples, they fail completely due to severe overfitting to background noise."

The Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) Paradigm

Instead of relying on human labels, the neural network pre-trains on abundant unlabeled factory camera scans through automated pretext tasks.

1. SimCLR (Contrastive Learning)

Pulls representations of positive augmented views together while pushing away negative images in the mini-batch via the NT-Xent Loss.

2. BYOL (Bootstrap Your Own Latent)

Non-contrastive learning without negative pairs. Uses an online predictor and Exponential Moving Average (EMA) target network to prevent collapse.

Domain-Adapted Industrial Augmentations

Standard ImageNet augmentations fail in manufacturing because natural objects follow gravity constraints (cars sit on roads). Industrial defect physics are fundamentally different:

360° Rotation Invariance

Metallic surface defects have no canonical orientation. Scratches and roll scales appear at arbitrary angles across the strip.

Photometric Contrast Jitter

Factory roller oil sheens and AC lighting oscillations create exposure fluctuations; our pipeline trains the model to ignore lighting variations.

Sensor Blur & Noise Simulation

Conveyor table mechanical vibrations cause optical defocus; Gaussian blur kernels simulate sensor defocus on the line.

Ablation Finding: Omitting 360-degree rotation invariance causes a 14.2% drop in downstream classification accuracy, proving rotation invariance is crucial for metallic defect inspection.
Speaker Script: "A critical contribution of our work is adapting data augmentations to industrial physics. Hot-rolled steel strip defects possess no canonical orientation. We implemented arbitrary 360-degree rotation invariance, multi-scale zooming, and optical sensor noise, ensuring the learned representations are robust against factory vibration and lighting oscillations."

Unsupervised Anomaly Detection (AUROC)

Feature Memory Bank Methodology:

  • Zero Negative Defect Labels Required: Stores 512-dimensional representations of nominal, flawless steel surfaces.
  • k-NN Metric: Computes mean cosine distance to the k-nearest neighbors (k=5) in the reference bank.
  • AUROC Score: Threshold-independent evaluation separating normal surfaces from anomalous defect textures.

Explainable AI: Autonomous Defect Localization (Grad-CAM)

Plant operators require visual verification before diverting steel sheets off the conveyor. Black-box predictions are unacceptable.

Mathematical Formulation

Computes gradients of predicted defect score with respect to activation maps of layer4: αkc = (1/Z) ∑ ∂yc / ∂Ak.

  • Zero Bounding Box Supervision: Accurately localizes scratches, crazing cracks, and pitted areas.
  • Operator Transparency: High-confidence spatial heatmaps confirm where defect pixels reside.
